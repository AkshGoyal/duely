The idea

Claude supplies the judgement. Duely supplies the memory.

Clients pay late, and chasing them is awkward, manual and easy to forget. Nobody tracks who promised to pay when. Claude is good at the awkward part: choosing a tone and writing a polite but firm message. It can't remember that Acme promised to pay last Tuesday and didn't, and it can't host a page the client can pay from. Duely does exactly those things.

Claude (the customer's own)

  • Understands "Acme owes me ₹85k, due 20 Oct"
  • Writes every reminder, matching its tone to the history
  • Decides who to chase today and how firmly

Duely (no model inside)

  • Stores invoices, amounts in paise, dates in IST
  • Hosts a public pay page with a UPI QR and one-tap client replies
  • Logs every event: viewed, promised, paid, disputed, nudged
  • Hands that memory back to Claude with a next_step_hint

Architecture

One service, three audiences

The core loop

The 5-beat demo

  1. Claude creates something real

    "Acme Studios owes me ₹85,000 for the October retainer, INV-104, due 20 Oct. Create it in Duely and draft a WhatsApp nudge, firm but friendly."

    create_invoice → save_nudge: Claude writes the message; Duely appends the pay link and returns a ready wa.me link.

  2. The client replies from their phone

    The pay page shows ₹85,000, "22 days overdue", a UPI button and QR. The client taps I'll pay by a date. No login, no app.

  3. The owner sees it and edits

    The dashboard shows Acme as Promised. The owner changes the amount to ₹80,000; the timeline records "₹85,000 → ₹80,000, on web".

  4. Claude decides who to chase

    "Who owes me money and what should I do today?"

    get_receivables_summary: broken promises first, then disputes, then days overdue × amount, each with a suggested action. Claude knows Acme has promised and holds off.

  5. Proof it's a business

    /metrics computes the north star and six supporting metrics live from the event log.

The MCP surface

Seven tools; the descriptions are the prompt

Each tool description is written as carefully as product copy, because it's the only instruction Claude gets. Every response carries the computed memory (days overdue, promise broken, last client action, nudges sent) plus a short next_step_hint. Bad input returns a sentence Claude can act on, such as "Due date must be a real calendar date in YYYY-MM-DD format".

Memory

Everything is an event

Four tables: users, invoices, nudges and an append-only events log. Money is stored as integer paise and shown in Indian grouping (₹1,25,000). "Days overdue" is counted from today in Asia/Kolkata. A promise is broken when its date has passed and the invoice is still unpaid.

Each event records who caused it, so the timeline reads like a story and the metrics can tell a client's action from Claude's.

Trust

How the constraints are met

No AI inside

  • No model SDKs, keys or LLM calls; the brief's grep returns nothing
  • All client-facing text arrives as tool arguments from the customer's Claude
  • Priority and suggested actions are plain sorting rules

Safety basics

  • 21+ character random tokens for pay links and connectors, never logged
  • All user and Claude text escaped by default in HTML
  • Rate-limited pay-page replies and login; bcrypt passwords; secure cookies
  • Review pass fixed an open redirect and a payment-date overwrite
